What Should You Know First?

A senior tub-to-shower conversion replaces the tub barrier with a lower-entry shower system while reusing the room efficiently. The best projects coordinate the base, plumbing, walls, seating, support, drainage, and finish repairs in one written scope.

This guide is for families replacing an unused or difficult bathtub with a more practical daily shower. It uses plain language, real planning questions, and current primary references. It does not assume that one product, threshold, or layout is best for every user.

01

Why the tub wall becomes the deciding issue

Many people can stand and bathe once inside a tub but struggle with the high step during entry and exit. A conversion targets that transition. It should also address the moments before and after bathing, including where a walker rests, where towels are reached, and how the person dries without standing on a wet floor.

02

Measure the existing alcove correctly

Record wall-to-wall width at the front and back, depth, ceiling height, plumbing-wall location, drain position, window trim, and adjacent fixtures. Note whether measurements are to finished surfaces. Older rooms are rarely perfectly square, so a single tape measurement is not enough for ordering.

03

What demolition can reveal

Removing a tub may expose water damage, weak subfloor, outdated valves, corroded piping, nonstandard framing, or an unexpected slab condition. A good agreement explains how concealed conditions are documented, priced, and approved instead of pretending they cannot occur.

04

Choosing the replacement base

The base must fit the opening, drain strategy, subfloor, and entry goal. Compare threshold height, finished dimensions, flange details, support method, drain options, surface texture, and compatibility with walls. A five-foot label does not mean every 60-inch alcove is identical.

05

Wall systems versus tile

Wall panels can reduce seams and installation time. Tile provides broad design flexibility but requires a carefully executed waterproof assembly. The decision should account for cleaning ability, installation time, wall condition, accessory mounting, long-term maintenance, and repair access.

06

Plumbing and control improvements

A conversion is a logical time to replace an old valve, add pressure balancing or thermostatic control where appropriate, relocate controls for reach, and add a handheld spray. Ask whether supply lines, drain, trap, and venting will be inspected or modified.

07

Building in support before walls close

Photograph all wall blocking with measurements from fixed reference points. Reinforce areas for the planned seat, grab bars, and possible future accessories. Do not assume a decorative wall panel can carry structural loads without suitable backing and approved fasteners.

08

One-day installation: when it may fit

A coordinated system and experienced crew may complete a straightforward conversion quickly. The schedule depends on product readiness, room access, plumbing, substrate, permit requirements, and hidden damage. Present one-day installation as available for qualifying projects, not guaranteed before evaluation.

09

DIY drop-ship planning

A capable contractor or experienced homeowner may prefer a shipped kit. Verify receiving access, freight inspection, storage, model details, installation documents, and required specialty materials. The installer should review instructions before the demolition date and confirm that the floor and drain plan are feasible.

10

Scope and price comparison

Compare bids line by line: demolition, haul-away, base, walls, valve, drain, labor, permits, trim, flooring repair, paint, accessories, warranty responsibility, and concealed conditions. A lower total may simply omit work that another contractor included.

11

Preparing the household

Plan bathing alternatives, clear the work path, protect pets, remove medications and valuables, and confirm water shutoff timing. Ask how dust and debris will be contained. After completion, request a walkthrough covering cleaning, sealant care, drain access, seat operation, and warranty contacts.
