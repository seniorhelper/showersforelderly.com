What is the best shower for an elderly person?

The best shower for an elderly person is the one that removes the hardest movement in their routine. For most families that means one of five options: grab bars and a seat in the existing tub, a low-threshold walk-in shower, a roll-in shower (threshold 1/2 inch or less), a 36 x 36 inch transfer shower, or a walk-in tub.

The step over a bathtub wall is where most bathing trouble starts. In the CDC's national look at bathroom injuries, 81.1% were caused by falls and 68.3% happened in or near the tub or shower, and the injury rate climbed from 58.8 per 100,000 people aged 15 to 24 to 515.3 per 100,000 at 85 and older. More than one in four adults 65 and older falls each year. No bathing product eliminates the risk of falls; products are designed to reduce risk when installed and used properly.

So the question is not "what is the nicest senior shower," it is "which movement do we need to take away?" Use the five options below, the fit finder, and the cost table to narrow it down in a few minutes.

These names get used loosely online. Here is what each one physically is, who it suits, and what to check.

A common planning mistake is choosing the lowest curb available and forgetting the seat, or putting the valve where a seated person cannot reach it without getting sprayed. Entry height matters, but so do seat height, bar placement and where the water comes on.

What heights and sizes should a senior shower have?

The 2010 ADA Standards were written for public buildings, but their numbers are the clearest published reference for an accessible shower at home. Use them as a starting point and adjust for the person.

  • Roll-in size: 30 x 60 inches minimum inside for a standard roll-in, 36 x 60 for the alternate layout. Many 60 inch tub alcoves can take one.
  • Threshold: 1/2 inch maximum for a roll-in; anything over 1/4 inch must be beveled.
  • Seat: top at 17 to 19 inches; folding seats save floor space but need wall reinforcement.
  • Grab bars: 33 to 36 inches above the floor, mounted into blocking, not tile anchors.
  • Handheld shower: hose at least 59 inches, so a seated person can rinse without standing.

What should you check before you choose?

  1. Watch one normal bath. Where does the person grab? Where do they hesitate? That is the movement to design out.
  2. Measure the space. Tub alcove width and length, the bathroom door's clear width, and where the drain sits.
  3. Decide on sitting. If they sit now, or might soon, plan the seat and a handheld shower from day one.
  4. Place the controls. The valve should be reachable from the seat and from outside the spray.
  5. Ask about funding before you sign. VA, Medicaid waiver and Medicare Advantage approvals usually have to come first.

Senior shower questions

What is the best type of shower for elderly adults?

It depends on how the person moves. Someone steady on their feet often needs only grab bars, a seat and a handheld shower. A walker or cane user does well with a low-threshold walk-in shower. Anyone who uses a wheelchair or a rolling shower chair needs a roll-in shower with a threshold of 1/2 inch or less.

How much does a shower for an elderly person cost?

Installed pricing for a roll-in shower, handicap shower or walk-in tub depends on the bathroom, the plumbing and the model you choose, so Aging Safely Baths gives a free, itemized written quote by phone or from photos. Grab bars, a seat and a handheld shower cost far less but do not lower the tub wall.

Does Medicare pay for a walk-in shower for seniors?

Original Medicare generally does not pay for walk-in showers or other bathroom remodels. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer bathroom safety benefits, Medicaid waivers may cover home modifications, and veterans may qualify for the VA HISA grant of up to $6,800. We help you check what you may qualify for.

Is a walk-in shower or a walk-in tub better for seniors?

A shower is usually better for someone who uses a walker or wheelchair or needs a caregiver to help, because there is nothing to wait for and nothing to climb into. A walk-in tub suits someone who wants to soak and can sit safely while it fills and drains.

What height should a shower seat be for an elderly person?

The ADA reference is a seat top 17 to 19 inches above the shower floor, close to a standard chair. A seat the person can sit on and stand up from without pulling on a towel bar is the right height, so test it before it is mounted.

Can a bathtub be converted to a senior shower in one day?

In most homes, yes. A one-piece or multi-piece system replaces the tub in the same space, usually in one day. A factory-certified tech does a full site visit before install day, so drain, floor or wall surprises are found before the work starts.
