Direct answer

The short answer

For most older adults a shower bench works best 17 to 19 inches high (the ADA range for shower seats), 15 to 16 inches deep, rated to hold at least 250 pounds, and placed so the controls and handheld shower are reachable without standing. Built-in or folding wall seats suit remodels; freestanding chairs suit renters.

What height should a shower bench be for a senior?

The ADA sets the top of a shower seat at 17 to 19 inches above the shower floor (section 610.3), and the same range for bathtub seats (610.2). It's a good starting point because it's close to the height of an ordinary chair, which is what most people are used to rising from.

The quickest home test: find the chair the person stands up from most easily, without pushing on the armrests, and measure its seat height. If it is inside 17 to 19 inches, match it. If it's higher (common for tall adults or after hip or knee surgery), an adjustable-leg freestanding seat lets you match it exactly. A seat that's too low is the usual mistake: rising from a low, wet seat is hard on knees and hips.

How deep and wide should the seat be?

The ADA gives two seat shapes for showers (section 610.3):

  • Rectangular seat: front edge 15 to 16 inches from the seat wall, rear edge no more than 2½ inches from the back wall, side edge no more than 1½ inches from the adjacent wall.
  • L-shaped seat: front edge 14 to 15 inches from the wall, with the "L" ending 22 to 23 inches from the main seat wall.

In a 36 by 36 inch transfer shower, the seat runs from the back wall to within 3 inches of the entry, so it can be reached straight from a wheelchair. Small gaps to the walls are deliberate: no space for a hand or a washcloth to drop into.

Where should the bench sit relative to the controls?

The person should be able to turn the water on, set the temperature and grab the handheld shower while seated, before the spray hits them. In a transfer shower the ADA puts the controls on the wall opposite the seat, 38 to 48 inches high and no more than 15 inches from the seat's centerline toward the opening (608.5.1). In a roll-in shower with a seat, controls go on the back wall next to the seat wall, no more than 27 inches from it (608.5.2). The hand shower hose should be at least 59 inches long (608.6); our handheld shower guide covers placement.

How much weight should a shower bench hold?

The ADA requires shower seats and their mounting to withstand 250 pounds of force at any point (610.4). For a freestanding chair, read the maker's rated capacity and compare it with the user's weight with a margin, since people drop onto seats rather than lowering slowly. For a folding wall seat, the rating only holds if the wall behind it was built to carry it; that's the same backing question as for grab bars.

Will insurance pay for a shower bench?

Original Medicare generally won't. Its national coverage list (NCD 280.1) lists bath seats as "Deny, comfort or convenience item; hygienic equipment." Some Medicare Advantage plans cover bathroom safety items, and state Medicaid waivers may include a seat as part of a bathroom modification. A built-in or folding seat that's part of a roll-in shower may also fall under the VA HISA grant for veterans who qualify. We help you check; nothing is guaranteed.

Questions families ask

What is the best height for a shower bench for an elderly person?

17 to 19 inches from the shower floor to the top of the seat is the ADA range and suits most adults. Match the chair the person rises from most easily; taller adults or people recovering from hip or knee surgery may need the higher end or an adjustable seat.

Is a folding shower seat as strong as a built-in bench?

It can be, if it is rated for the load and fastened into solid backing. The ADA standard for shower seats is 250 pounds at any point. A folding seat screwed into tile over drywall without backing will not reach its rating.

How deep should a shower seat be?

For a rectangular seat, the ADA puts the front edge 15 to 16 inches from the seat wall. An L-shaped seat is 14 to 15 inches deep at the front edge. Deeper seats make it harder to get the feet under the body to stand.

Should a shower bench be inside or outside the spray?

Inside the shower area, but positioned so the controls and handheld shower can be reached from the seat before the water is turned on. In transfer showers the controls sit on the wall opposite the seat; in roll-ins, on the back wall near the seat wall.

Does Medicare cover shower benches?

Original Medicare generally does not; its coverage list denies bath seats as comfort or convenience items. Some Medicare Advantage plans, Medicaid waivers and the VA HISA grant may help in qualifying cases.
